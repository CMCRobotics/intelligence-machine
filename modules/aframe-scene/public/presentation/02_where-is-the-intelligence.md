## Plants

They do not have brains or neurons like animals...

but many researchers argue they show a kind of distributed intelligence. <!-- .element class="fragment"-->

--

## Plants

<p>
Roots can detect water, nutrients, toxins, and even obstacles, then “decide” which way to grow.
</p>
<p>
Plants send chemical signals through the air (volatile organic compounds) and underground via fungal networks (the “wood-wide web”).
</p>

--

## Plants 

<p>
Some plants “remember” stress (like drought) through epigenetic changes, which helps them adapt faster if it happens again.</p>
<p>
A climbing plant will “choose” which support to grow toward, adjusting based on touch and light.
</p>

--

## Plants

This intelligence is not centralized in a brain—it is embodied in their whole physiology, especially in the root tips, which Charles Darwin once compared to a “brain-like” structure because they sense and coordinate growth.

--

## Computers

Their intelligence is located in some physical parts (depending of how they are built to make decisions).
